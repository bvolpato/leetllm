# Watch a revised lesson

Start by inspecting the numbered frames alone, before reading the article or the editor's notes. Ask what changed on screen and what visual encoding made it clear. Whenever a frame poses a decision, pause before advancing, note your reasoning and the details legible in that still, or record what information is missing, then identify the first frame that reveals the outcome.

Next, read the spoken lines alongside those frames to confirm that the picture and narration pose the same question and hold steady while the viewer thinks. Verify that frames avoid prose slides, fictional-data disclaimers, and unrelated roleplay, and check that any static poster image agrees with its caption. Read the article afterward to verify technical consistency, keeping evidence visible in static frames distinct from timing and audio cues that require continuous playback.

Review the teaching arc, not just isolated frames. Explain the central mechanism, reconstruct the worked example, and reason through one small changed case using the film alone. Identify where terminology, a caveat, or an unrelated branch interrupts understanding before the example reaches its result. Welcome useful analogies and imaginative visual constructions; check the mapping to the actual mathematics instead of rejecting them for being informal.

Compare the selected film with the current article, reader chapter, quiz, and next-lesson handoff. Distinguish coverage checks, source review, encoded visual inspection, and continuous playback with sound. Report what remains unverified; a clean script score or attractive still is not a complete teaching review.
