# Serving Benchmark Engineering: manual source notes

Checked September 23, 2026. The lesson's six-request data, operating curve, timing worksheet, and uncertainty examples are synthetic or calculated. They are not model, client, GPU, or production measurements. The downloadable program scores normalized token events; it doesn't generate load, parse SSE, judge response semantics, infer stable capacity, or approve deployment.

## Sources and scope

- [Current vLLM benchmark CLI](https://docs.vllm.ai/en/stable/benchmarking/cli/): checked purpose, latency, load controls, cache warning, and timed-trace selections. It recommends GuideLLM for production benchmarking. Stream-output ITL and completion-based TPOT differ from this worksheet's token-event boundaries. Gamma burstiness isn't a universal traffic model.
- [Current serving benchmark implementation](https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm/blob/main/vllm/benchmarks/serve.py): inspected request generation, successful-request TPOT/gap accounting, semaphore waiting, queue-inclusive calculation, and metric reporting. Constant-rate gaps are rescaled to a final scheduled arrival of N/rate; self-timed replay bypasses random/ramp controls. Queue summaries require a client cap and cover successes. Queue-inclusive E2EL also requires finite request rate. Failures need separate offered-request accounting.
- [MLPerf Inference rules](https://github.com/mlcommons/inference_policies/blob/master/inference_rules.adoc): read scenario definitions, early-stopping caveats, scheduled-to-reply latency boundary, and Server search behavior. Single Stream waits for completion; Server/Interactive uses Poisson arrivals. The benchmark supplies scenario, workload, quality, duration, and uncertainty rules. Its names don't establish compliance for a custom test. No claim that this exercise is an MLPerf result or that its arbitrary SLO limits are MLPerf limits.
- [Little's original publisher abstract](https://doi.org/10.1287/opre.9.3.383): finite stationary means under the stated assumptions give L = lambda W. Apply the same system boundary to admitted arrival rate, mean residence time, and mean outstanding work. An average concurrency calculation doesn't bound burst concurrency.
- [BurstGPT v5 abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.17644): real workload characteristics include concurrency, conversations, prompt/response lengths, and failures. Its service population doesn't become representative of another application by being public. Retained the 2025 revision year; didn't introduce its aggregate trace counts as requirements or local observations.
- [ServeGen NSDI 2026 author abstract](https://www.usenix.org/conference/nsdi26/presentation/xiang-servegen) and [authors' README](https://github.com/alibaba/ServeGen/blob/main/README.md): per-client workload composition includes language, multimodal, and reasoning classes. The repository supplies rate/data distributions and privacy-preserving hashed conversation content. It doesn't reproduce missing application prompts or guarantee representative traffic for this lesson's six-row fixture.
- [Etalon v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.07000v2): read sections 2.2, 3.1, 4.1 including Algorithm 1, and the deadline-selection limits. The metric accumulates slack, counts playback deadlines crossed by a stall, and resets subsequent deadlines after late output. A late-token fraction or simple fixed-deadline comparison isn't the published fluidity algorithm. First-token target fitting and scheduling slack are choices with acknowledged limits; no universal reading-speed requirement was taken from it.
- [DistServe v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2401.09670v3): checked the introduction's explicit per-GPU goodput definition and rate-search description. It means the maximum rate meeting declared TTFT/TPOT attainment constraints per provisioned GPU, distinct from completed qualified requests divided by a finite cohort's drain-inclusive horizon. Reused the preceding disaggregation reviews; didn't repeat the entire paper or publish its historical ratios as current guarantees.
- [NIST proportion confidence intervals](https://www.itl.nist.gov/div898/handbook/prc/section2/prc241.htm): read the full page's Wilson formula, one-sided adaptation, exact-binomial alternative, and terminology correction. The worksheet uses a one-sided 95% Wilson lower bound for independent, constant-probability Bernoulli outcomes at one preselected load. Observed attainment, interval confidence, selection after a sweep, and correlated traffic are separate issues.

These are relevant primary selections, not complete upstream projects or every paper's full appendices. The current CLI documentation is a moving target; pin the actual installed version and measurement adapter in a benchmark record.

## Corrections and checks

- Refreshed the dated CLI controls and added current client-queue metrics and benchmark-purpose guidance.
- Made the current CLI's stream/chunk and terminal-completion definitions explicit before comparing them with the token-event worksheet.
- Expanded the fluidity explanation beyond merely resetting deadlines: stalls can cross multiple playback deadlines.
- Added a computed uncertainty counterexample: 190/200 gives 95% observed attainment but a 91.8% one-sided 95% Wilson lower bound. Even 20/20 gives a lower bound of only 88.1%. These calculations don't handle correlated bursts or selecting the best noisy sweep point.
- Reproduced the shared chart's marker clamping: its 100% observations were drawn below the 100% gridline. The local plot now maps coordinates exactly and retains the 95% target and synthetic-data scope.
- Reproduced oversized-integer timestamp OverflowError and an accepted positive arrival window that underflowed to zero seconds, causing ZeroDivisionError. The scorer now rejects unsupported numeric ranges and nonfinite derived offered rates as malformed input. Focused regression cases cover these failures; the ordinary six-request results remain unchanged.

## Generated claims removed

The old research packet confused ITL with variance, supplied an unsupported universal reading-speed floor, banned repeated prompts, assumed all continuous-batching engines deduplicate cache, required Poisson arrivals for all real traffic, called ShareGPT the universal standard, prescribed fixed chat SLOs, equated a goodput peak with a release boundary, promised monotonic throughput/latency curves, blamed correct speculative sampling for inherent quality loss, and declared synchronous Python inevitably the bottleneck. Those are not general facts. Its stock outline and unverified tool prescriptions weren't retained.
