A **restricted stock unit (RSU)** is an employer's promise to deliver company shares, or their cash equivalent, after stated vesting conditions are met. Usable value arrives after the employee satisfies the vesting conditions, such as remaining employed through a vesting date, and accounts for any tax withholding.

## Quoted value and liquidity

A grant's quoted value is usually calculated from a share price at some reference date. The actual value at vest can rise or fall with the stock. Public-company shares may be tradable after settlement; private-company shares can remain illiquid even after vesting, depending on company policy, securities rules, and available liquidity events.

## Example

An employer offers 400 RSUs worth \$100 each at grant, for a stated \$40,000 package. If one quarter vests after the first year and the share price is then \$80, the first 100 shares are worth \$8,000 before withholding, not the \$10,000 implied by the original quote. Unvested units may be forfeited when employment ends.
