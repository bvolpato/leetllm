# Manual evidence review: Guardrails and Safety Filters

Checked September 22, 2026. This replaces the July generated packet, whose schema example claimed unattached validators were active and whose latency discussion implied universal cost and detection behavior. Article examples exercise local routing and contracts; they don't benchmark a classifier or deploy a security boundary.

- NeMo's current reference includes input, retrieval, dialog, execution, and output rails. Its output streaming configuration lists `stream_first: true`, so enabling rails doesn't establish review before release. Engine and integration behavior must be checked: https://docs.nvidia.com/nemo/guardrails/latest/configure-guardrails/configuration-reference
- Guardrails AI validators must be explicitly configured, with their own criteria and failure actions. A field description isn't a detector. Current docs also deprecate in-code validator installation in favor of PyPI packages: https://guardrailsai.com/guardrails/docs/concepts/validators
- Protect AI's English DeBERTa-v3 injection classifier is marked archived and unmaintained. Its sample truncates at 512 tokens; historical evaluation metrics aren't guarantees on product traffic: https://huggingface.co/protectai/deberta-v3-base-prompt-injection-v2
- Prompt Guard 2 targets attempted instruction override, distinct from harm moderation. Its 22M/86M backbone variants use 512-token windows. The model card's A100/512-token classification times are 19.3/92.4 ms, not request percentiles. Adaptive and application-specific attacks remain limitations; segmented checks don't guarantee contextual coverage: https://huggingface.co/meta-llama/Llama-Prompt-Guard-2-86M
- Llama Guard 4 is a dense 12B text-and-image safety model that generates labels and categories. Its taxonomy isn't arbitrary brand policy, and its own model card warns about prompt attacks against the classifier: https://huggingface.co/meta-llama/Llama-Guard-4-12B
- Presidio's analyzer combines recognizers and configurable NLP engines; spaCy is the documented default and transformer NER is optional. Detection and configured anonymizer transformations are separate steps. Local display tokens aren't universal entity identifiers: https://presidio.dataprivacystack.org/analyzer/ and https://presidio.dataprivacystack.org/anonymizer/
- TRUE reports strong, complementary NLI and question-generation/answering results across eleven datasets. It doesn't establish perfect logical verification or that lexical/embedding methods miss every subtle distinction: https://aclanthology.org/2022.naacl-main.287/
- SelfCheckGPT investigates sample-based factual inconsistency without external evidence. The article's exact-string overlap exercise is a separate fixture; unanimous repetition can be wrong and low overlap can reflect paraphrases: https://aclanthology.org/2023.emnlp-main.557/
- Indirect prompt injection enters through external content. Delimiters and escaped markup aren't permission boundaries, and a synthetic exact-marker canary can miss partial, encoded, and other disclosures: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.12173 and https://genai.owasp.org/llmrisk/llm01-prompt-injection/
- Zou et al. demonstrate historical transferable adversarial suffixes against aligned models, not a measurement of every present classifier: https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.15043
- OpenAI's current moderation guide distinguishes supported text/image categories, warns that changing models can require score recalibration, and says integrated streaming moderation scores arrive after complete generation: https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/moderation
- Granite Guardian's paper covers harm and RAG support/relevance risks; ShieldGemma 2 is a 4B image-safety model. These are examples to evaluate, not a universal leaderboard: https://arxiv.org/html/2412.07724v1 and https://ai.google.dev/gemma/docs/shieldgemma/model_card_2
- Idempotency depends on stable request identity and durable atomic state under an executor contract. A key alone doesn't reconcile a timeout or undo an external effect. Relevant AWS contract passages were checked in the previous agent review: https://aws.amazon.com/builders-library/making-retries-safe-with-idempotent-APIs/
- The Commission's July 27, 2026 notice reports AI Omnibus entry into force and the December 2, 2027 Annex III / August 2, 2028 Annex I high-risk dates. This doesn't defer every provision: https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/news/ai-omnibus-enters-force
- Official Service Desk reproductions of 2024 Articles 19 and 26 cover purpose-appropriate logs under provider/deployer control, at least six months unless applicable law provides otherwise. Those reproductions aren't a claim to have fetched the full amended legal text; EUR-Lex's consolidated page presented a browser verification challenge during this review: https://ai-act-service-desk.ec.europa.eu/en/ai-act/article-19 and https://ai-act-service-desk.ec.europa.eu/en/ai-act/article-26
- NIST AI RMF is voluntary risk-management guidance: https://www.nist.gov/itl/ai-risk-management-framework

Manual arithmetic: 100 attacks among 10,100 requests is about 0.99% prevalence. At exactly 0.1% prevalence, 1% FPR, and 90% recall, expected false blocks per true block are 11.1. No industry prevalence, acceptable false-pause rate, automatic leak prevention, or universal guard latency is inferred from these fixtures.
