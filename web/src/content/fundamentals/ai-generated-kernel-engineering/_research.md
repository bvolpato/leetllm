# Manual source review, September 22, 2026

- Reviewed the complete article, CPU evaluators, questions, citations, generated research, native illustration, and banner. Removed invented launch/bandwidth outcomes, categorical model limitations, and guarantees of zero-cost shadow execution or immediate recovery.
- [PyTorch 2.14 operator registration](https://docs.pytorch.org/docs/2.14/library.html): opcheck tests registration/schema/fake-tensor/compilation integration. Numerical semantics and gradients need separate checks. The teaching evaluator isn't a sandbox or a proof over the whole domain.
- [PyTorch 2.14 assert_close](https://docs.pytorch.org/docs/2.14/testing.html#torch.testing.assert_close): equal_nan defaults False; signed infinities must agree. The executable tolerance exercise shows absolute tolerance accepting zero, and direct FP32 squaring losing a large finite normalization input.
- [Compute Sanitizer](https://docs.nvidia.com/compute-sanitizer/ComputeSanitizer/index.html): initcheck defaults to global memory; current shared/all modes are explicit. Memcheck precedes narrower checks. Clean instrumented runs don't prove every path, concurrency pattern, or deadlock safe.
- [Nsight Compute stall reasons](https://docs.nvidia.com/nsight-compute/ProfilingGuide/index.html): long scoreboard concerns L1TEX dependencies; short scoreboard concerns MIO dependencies, including shared memory, special math, and branching. Samples attach to waiting consumers. Address/bank/traffic evidence is needed before prescribing a change.
- [KernelAgent's March 6, 2026 description](https://pytorch.org/blog/kernelagent-hardware-guided-gpu-kernel-optimization-via-multi-agent-orchestration/): hardware-guided optimization of Triton inference kernels. Described workflow passages read; benchmark claims aren't general production guarantees.
- [Original KernelBench](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.10517v1): 250 workloads with public task shape/input generators. Its benchmark domain differs from a general-purpose library operator's accepted domain.
- [KernelBench-Verified methods](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.16241v1): H200, single-turn best-of-five, 247 active tasks, fixed shapes, four value transformations, and peak allocated-memory metrics. Alternate distributions gate correctness; timing remains on the original distribution. TF32's numerical policy must suit the application.
- [KernelBench-X methods](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.04956v1): 176 tasks, 15 categories, precision/quantization variants, normal/outlier tests, six GPUs. Its hardware-efficiency quantities use idealized task FLOPs/bytes rather than executed instructions or measured HBM traffic.
- [SOL-ExecBench methods](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.19173v1): 235 B200 problems from 124 models. SOLAR estimates hardware/fusion bounds from traced operations and shapes; SOL score also uses a fixed software baseline. Value-dependent optimizations and hardware variability can require bound review.
- [CUDA 13.4 runtime errors](https://docs.nvidia.com/cuda/cuda-runtime-api/cuda_runtime_api/group__CUDART__TYPES.html): illegal-address errors require process termination/relaunch. Disabling dispatch doesn't repair that process or undo corrupted state. Recovery, retries, and flag propagation need their own tests.
- Replaced fabricated successful hashes/timings/signature with an unsigned, incomplete receipt template. Added CPU policy/traffic exercises and failure/expanded-view cases to the actual evaluator. These aren't CUDA execution, hardware profiling, malicious-code isolation, or rollout evidence.
- Reused preceding bundles' checked compiler, local/shared spilling, warp-sector, timing, and cache passages. Relevant primary selections read, not complete upstream projects or every paper. Inspect final illustrations in both themes and retain explicit coverage limits.
